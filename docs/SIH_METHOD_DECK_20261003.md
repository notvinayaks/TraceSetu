## What the deck actually claims

The method combines externally sourced service assertions, a narrow rule-based deposit-pattern experiment and chronological breadth-first search. It does not derive company names from graph topology alone. The app traces sourced labels; the deposit-candidate detector remains a separate, unintegrated research reference.

The six slides cover the exact SIH title and team metadata; the unknown-address problem and source of service names; repeated token/fee/sweep checks, reconciled integer balances and graph-search states; a scoped 36-hour sprint on the existing foundation and Ministry deployment path; an evidence-removal comparison and actual prototype screenshot; and primary references with public repository/video links.

## New review findings made explicit

- The service register is the necessary source of names. Public graph patterns alone cannot establish a real-world operator or deposit role.
- GraphSense provides source-linked public attribution tags. A reviewed live import is proposed, not implemented here. Historical source dates are not historical ownership intervals.
- Etherscan address metadata requires eligible paid access. It cannot be counted as a free operational label feed. Current code treats nametags as hypotheses with unestablished roles.
- Matching a fee-funding/sweep pattern can generate a lead. A customer can imitate all observed events, so a match never becomes proof of control by itself.
- A hot-wallet assertion does not establish a direct-deposit role. Chronological reachability does not establish the same money units across account-based transfers.
- The six parsers, separate research experiment, historical public Bitcoin check and official SAHYOG gate remain distinct. The public check found no VASP; exports remain NOT_SENT.

## Evaluator criteria

**Novelty:** the proposed contribution is the evidence-driven decision workflow: retain nearer unresolved custody leads, challenge a source without erasing history and direct the next bounded acquisition towards evidence that may change the result. Graphs, labels and CRUD are not claimed as novel. Comparative validation is still required.

**Feasibility:** the 36-hour schedule uses the existing foundation and targets one bounded public-data case through reviewed export. It is a plan, not a measured build time, six-chain accuracy claim or national deployment commitment.

**Practicality:** separate API/workers, durable PostgreSQL jobs, budgets, caching and case permissions are a foundation. Shared quotas, agency fairness, SSO/MFA, load/fault evaluation, governed labels and approved SAHYOG integration remain required.

**Stack:** Python/FastAPI performs the evidence rules and API work; React/TypeScript/Cytoscape provides investigator review and graph display; PostgreSQL and workers run durable jobs, with SQLite locally; SHA-256/Ed25519 protect export integrity. These tools do not themselves establish attribution accuracy.
