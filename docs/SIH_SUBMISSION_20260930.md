## What the deck explains

The proposed method starts with sourced service-wallet identities. Repeated token collection and service funding of transaction fees can suggest an unlabelled deposit address. Those matches remain hypotheses. A temporal graph search follows recorded transfers in ledger order to supported custody boundaries and exposes earlier unresolved candidates. Source challenge and bounded next queries help an investigator decide which evidence to obtain next.

The working prototype includes case tracing, source challenge, query budgets and signed evidence export. Its six read-only chain adapters have uneven provider coverage. The new deposit detector is a separate research reference with 64 synthetic tests, not integrated live attribution. Independent evaluation and official SAHYOG connectivity remain open. The deck says this visibly rather than relying only on presenter notes.

The screenshot is an actual TraceSetu training-case interface captured on 26 September. The app design remains unchanged. Fictional wallets and services stay labelled as synthetic. The current app was inspected again on 30 September, including its saved public Bitcoin case showing no established VASP. That inspection did not perform a fresh blockchain acquisition. The supplied video demonstrates the earlier prototype, not the new research detector.
